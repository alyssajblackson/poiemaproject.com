Women's Health Education

A large part of Poiema Project is focused on the gap between the health decisions women are asked to make and how much they have actually been taught about their bodies.

I research women's health, menstrual health, and hormone education with a focus on making complex information easier to understand and use.

I am currently completing the Women's Hormone Health Certification Program through the Institute for Menstrual Health as I continue developing educational resources and future curriculum.

True Health

I designed True Health for True Homes, a homebuilding company with a distributed workforce across North Carolina and South Carolina. The program included a phased three year rollout, wellness incentives, health education, clinical vendor coordination, employee challenges, and a structure for connecting participation to the company's existing health plan.

Mold Remediation Policy Pitch

Alabama has no law requiring mold testing in university housing, even though the state ranks fourth in the country for mold risk. This pitch proposed a state policy requiring annual, independent mold and moisture testing in every Alabama university dorm before students move in, with mandatory remediation and clearance testing whenever a problem is found.

The proposal has five parts: full building testing every summer, independent ACAC or IAC2 certified inspectors, mandatory remediation before occupancy, post remediation clearance testing, and an annual health summary published for each campus. A phased two year rollout starts with the highest risk buildings before expanding statewide.

This pitch placed second in Samford's Policy Pitch Competition.

A Cycle Guide

This guide breaks the menstrual cycle into its four phases, menstrual, follicular, ovulatory, and luteal, and lays out what is happening in the body during each one, how it might affect mood and energy, what kind of food supports that phase, and how to adjust movement and exercise accordingly.

It is built to be a quick, practical reference for women who want to work with their cycle instead of against it.

The First Twenty Years

I wrote The First Twenty Years to look at the health issues, misconceptions, and gaps in care affecting girls and young women between ages 10 and 30.

The paper looks at menstrual health, cycle education, hormone care, nutrition, mental health, and opportunities for better education and support.
